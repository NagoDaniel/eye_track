## T13 experiment plan (agreed by Nágó Dániel, 2026-10-29), in order of expected gain per effort
Hypotheses: H1 head-pose features learned from an almost still head (standardised by a tiny calibration spread, so later head movement is amplified; head translation is not a regressor); H2 centre pull from ridge shrinkage plus noisy features, pushed by leave-one-dot-out CV towards strong regularisation; H3 weak vertical signal (iris y small, coupled with the lids); H4 lag partly a timestamp error (browser features use `t` at the frame callback, not `capture_time`, `frontend/src/face/faceTracker.ts`); H5 VP9 2.5 Mbit/s compression noise on the iris.
- E0 measurement harness: **done** (the lab).
- E1 diagnostics, **done 2026-10-30** (`make lab ARGS="diagnose"`, `docs/evidence/t13-lab/e1_diagnose.txt`): head-pose spread calibration vs playback; prediction split by feature group (start vs end centre rest); `t - capture_time` against best lag; alpha against slope, CV error, dot error.
  Results: (a) head angles stay inside their calibration spread in playback (median |z| 0.2 to 0.9 for yaw; yaw sd 2 to 10 degrees during calibration, so the head does turn towards the dots), but **face size and head height shift** by 1.5 to 3 calibration spreads (`ty` shifts the same way in all 10 sessions: a posture change between calibration and the video); (b) the drift between the first and the last centre rest (true gaze unchanged; up to 470 viewport px in y) comes from the **non-iris features**: lids (GG -276, FF -211, CC -219 px in y), head angles (BB -227 and -299, AA -178) and face size (GG -385, CC -215, EE +193, BB +167); the iris contributes at most about 100 px. The iris carries x (its contribution spreads 220 to 490 px in playback) but little y, so y depends on the lids and head, which also change for reasons other than gaze; (c) **H4 refuted**: `t - capture_time` is 4 to 15 ms in every session and unrelated to the 0 to 300 ms best lag, so the browser stamps frames promptly (sensor latency is not visible this way); (d) **H2 mostly refuted**: alpha from 0.01 to 30 changes the median pause error by less than 20 px and the x slope not at all (0.88 / 0.78); a weaker ridge raises the y slope a little (0.65 to 0.76 in study 7) but not the error; alpha 100 is clearly worse. All ten sessions use the linear model.
  **Reordered next steps** (after E1): E4 features first (no product change needed: the lab can pass a `PipelineSpec` with other feature columns): leave out face size, lids or head angles one at a time; add `tx, ty, tz`; a slowly adapting baseline for lid openness and face size (remove their slow drift, keep their fast gaze-related part). Then E7 (validation dots in the fit: they come later in time, so they teach the model the posture change) and E6 (drift correction per block). E2 (alpha) and E3 (timing) move to the end.

Leaving out the lids, the head angles or everything but the iris, and adding `tx, ty, tz`, make several sessions clearly worse. High-passing lids, face size or head angles helps the as-measured error (passes for `f-hp-all-30/60`, `f-hp-lidsface-60`) but not the drift-corrected one: both remove slow drift and do not add up. Dropping face size does not change the centre pull (slopes unchanged) and removes only a little of the end-of-video drift.
